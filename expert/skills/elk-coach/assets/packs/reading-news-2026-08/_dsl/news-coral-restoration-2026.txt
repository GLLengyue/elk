--- A
Coral restoration used to mean gardening. A damaged colony was broken into fragments, the fragments were fixed to the reef, and each regrew into a new colony. The method works well against local injury: anchor scars, blast fishing, plumes of sediment. It works badly against heat, because a fragment taken from a heat-sensitive parent will bleach again when the water warms. That distinction has become decisive. The Fourth Global Bleaching Event, tracked by NOAA and partners in more than fifty countries, is the largest on record, and the ocean has absorbed more than 90 per cent of the excess heat in the atmosphere.
--- B
The damage has been measured with unusual care. The Australian Institute of Marine Science has surveyed 124 reefs under a monitoring programme running since 1986, and judged the bleaching of the summer of 2024 the most severe and geographically extensive in those four decades, with 40 per cent of reefs recording their largest annual fall in coral cover. The event reversed five years of recovery. By June 2026 the park authority was still reporting coral disease on twenty reefs and low to moderate bleaching on eight others. The margin for error is thin: sea surface temperatures across the Marine Park have risen by 0.94 degrees Celsius since 1900, while corals live within one to two degrees of their thermal maximum.
--- C
Reefs repair themselves mainly by reproducing, and the arithmetic of that is unforgiving. Most corals spawn once or twice a year, releasing eggs and sperm on a few synchronised nights governed by the moon and the water temperature. After mass bleaching, the bare patches can be recolonised only if larvae settle on them and grow. In the open, however, most larvae are eaten or swept out to sea, and survival from egg to settled polyp is typically well under 1 per cent.
--- D
Restoration programmes therefore intervene at exactly that bottleneck. Teams collect the spawn slick from the surface, rear the larvae in floating nursery pools, and release them over damaged sections inside mesh enclosures known as seedboxes. Trials of the device developed by CSIRO, Australia's national science agency, and published in November 2025, recorded settlement up to 56 times greater than in unassisted natural conditions. In 2026 about 130 scientists and rangers gathered an estimated 14.6 million coral eggs during a single mass spawning on the Great Barrier Reef, reared them, and returned the first batches of juveniles to the reef.
--- E
The ambition now is to make this routine rather than seasonal. The Reef Restoration and Adaptation Program involves more than 300 scientists from over twenty institutions, and produced more than 35 million coral embryos in the past year, settled on tens of thousands of ceramic structures; its stated target is to place 100 million corals on the reef each year that survive to at least twelve months. Much of the rearing happens on land, at the National Sea Simulator, and a comparable laboratory on Praslin Island in the Seychelles recorded its first successful spawning, producing roughly 800,000 embryos from fourteen parent colonies. An eight-year research programme has also taught aquarium corals to spawn out of season, by offsetting light and temperature, which turns a single frantic annual harvest into a supply spread across much of the year.
--- F
Material is being changed as well as multiplied. Under the name of assisted gene flow, researchers identify colonies that resist bleaching at temperatures one to two degrees Celsius above the regional norm, often survivors of naturally warm microclimates, and use them as breeding stock, sometimes crossing them with local varieties to preserve diversity. Pilot sites report better survival through repeated bleaching when outplants carry heat-tolerant hosts or symbionts. The approach carries genuine objection: moving genotypes between regions risks outbreeding depression, and heat-adapted corals may be poorly matched to local algal partners. Governance frameworks from the NOAA Coral Reef Conservation Program therefore require provenance tracking, phased planting and multi-year monitoring before any scaling up. Elsewhere, printed calcium carbonate structures give larvae a stable surface on rubble fields where they would otherwise roll away.
--- G
The economic case is not in dispute. A valuation published in October 2025 put the reef's contribution to the Australian economy at approximately 9 billion dollars a year and 77,000 full-time jobs, with a total asset value of 95 billion dollars; the reef also absorbs wave energy that would otherwise reach the coast. Finance has begun to follow: parametric insurance policies now release funds automatically once satellite data confirms that bleaching stress has passed an agreed threshold, allowing repairs to begin within weeks rather than after a season of assessment.
--- H
None of this settles the argument about what restoration is for. At Lady Elliot Island, the cooling wake of a cyclone in February 2025 lowered water temperatures by about 2 degrees Celsius and roughly 90 per cent of the bleached coral there recovered without assistance, a reminder that reefs can still repair themselves given the chance. Researchers on the island warn that what defeats recovery is repetition: back-to-back events leave no interval in which to rebuild. Cedric Robillot, executive director of the restoration programme, is blunter. The work, he has said, relies on the premise that the world will act on emissions; if it does not, there is no point.

--- A
A small modular reactor is, in the definition used by the International Energy Agency, a fission plant rated at under 300 megawatts of electrical output, compared with 1,000 to 1,700 megawatts for the conventional reactors built in the 1970s and 1980s. The appeal rests on three claims. Because the units are smaller, they can in principle be assembled in a factory rather than poured on site. Because they are modular, capacity can be added in increments of 80 to 300 megawatts rather than in one enormous commitment. And because the footprint is modest, they can be sited closer to where the power is actually needed.
--- B
What has turned this from an engineering curiosity into a commercial proposition is demand from computing. The IEA counts plans of varying maturity for up to 25 gigawatts of SMR capacity worldwide, and its World Energy Outlook for 2025 recorded agreements and expressions of interest covering 30 gigawatts, most of it intended to run data centres. Google has signed for up to seven reactors from Kairos Power, together providing about 500 megawatts. For operators facing grid connection queues measured in years, a reactor next door solves an urgent problem that wind and solar, however cheap, do not.
--- C
The difficulty is price. Lazard's analysis puts first-generation SMR electricity at about $214 per megawatt-hour, while benchmarking by SMR Intel suggests a first-of-a-kind range of $80-150. The most instructive precedent is the Carbon Free Power Project in Idaho. When NuScale and a consortium of municipal utilities proposed it in 2016, the target price was $55 per megawatt-hour. By July 2023 it had reached $89, after an increase in construction cost of 75 per cent, from $5.3 billion to $9.3 billion. The partners terminated the scheme that November.
--- D
Several projects are now further advanced. Ontario Power Generation has approved the first BWRX-300 at Darlington at a cost of 7.7 billion Canadian dollars for 300 megawatts, an overnight figure of roughly $25,000 per kilowatt, with four units in total priced at 20.9 billion Canadian dollars. TerraPower's Natrium reactor, rated at 345 megawatts, broke ground in Wyoming in June 2024 with completion targeted for 2030. In Britain, the government selected the Rolls-Royce design in June 2025, named Wylfa in Wales as the site for three units totalling 1,440 megawatts, and pledged 2.5 billion pounds towards the programme.
--- E
Every one of these projections rests on the assumption that costs fall with repetition, the learning curve that transformed solar panels and lithium batteries. Rolls-Royce argues that its tenth reactor would produce power at between 40 and 75 pounds per megawatt-hour, comparable to British wind, but concedes that its own factory economics depend on winning substantial export orders. A study at the Technical University of Berlin, modelling uncertainty in production costs, put the mean levelised cost of a factory-built Rolls-Royce unit at approximately £163 per megawatt-hour, against £30-50 for British wind and solar today.
--- F
The export record offers little comfort. As of 2025, no SMR had been exported and operated commercially anywhere outside its country of origin. South Korea spent several hundred million dollars developing the 100-megawatt SMART reactor and found no international buyers. Argentina's CAREM project was abandoned in 2024 after a decade of construction. Russia's floating Akademik Lomonosov has been running since 2020, and China's Linglong One, a 125-megawatt unit built by CNNC, reached commercial operation in the first half of 2026, making it the only land-based SMR close to operating at scale.
--- G
Regulation has become faster without becoming simple. The ADVANCE Act of July 2024 streamlined licensing at the Nuclear Regulatory Commission and cut fees, and a subsequent executive order requires the agency to assess new applications within 18 months, a fraction of the decade such reviews once took. Design approval is not the same as permission to build, however: NuScale, the only developer with a certified American design, still has to clear separate construction-permit and operating-licence steps that can each take 30 months or more. No Generation III+ SMR in the United States has yet been delivered on time and to budget.
--- H
The gap between the two positions is really about what counts as evidence. The World Nuclear Industry Status Report for 2025 described the technology as a hypothesis with very uncertain timescales and costs. Jacopo Buongiorno of the Massachusetts Institute of Technology observed in 2025 that the capital needed to deploy even a single unit exceeds what most developers can raise. What would settle the argument is not another agreement but a finished reactor whose final bill can be compared with the estimate that preceded it.

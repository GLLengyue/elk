--- A
Messenger RNA vaccines were proved at industrial scale during the coronavirus pandemic, when products were designed, manufactured and distributed within a year. The harder test of the platform is now underway. On 19 August 2026, Merck and Moderna announced that a phase 3 trial had met its primary endpoints. The product, intismeran autogene, also known as mRNA-4157, is an mRNA construct carried in lipid nanoparticles that encodes up to thirty-four neoantigens drawn from an individual patient's own tumour. Given alongside pembrolizumab after surgery, it improved recurrence-free and distant metastasis-free survival in patients with completely resected stage IIB to IV melanoma. It was the first positive phase 3 result ever reported for an individualised neoantigen therapy.
--- B
The platform rests on two inventions. The first is nucleotide modification: substituting a modified nucleoside for uridine prevents the immune system from destroying the RNA before it can be read, a finding by Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman that earned them the Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine in 2023. The second is delivery. Naked RNA is fragile, so it is packaged in lipid nanoparticles, assemblies of an ionisable lipid, a helper lipid, cholesterol and a polyethylene glycol-modified lipid. A third advance has been mundane but consequential. Improvements in freeze-drying reported in 2026 allow some mRNA products to remain stable for long periods at 2 to 8 degrees Celsius, removing the ultra-cold chain that had constrained distribution to wealthy health systems.
--- C
Making a personalised vaccine is a sequence of four steps. Tissue from the patient's tumour, and usually a blood sample, is sequenced, and algorithms compare the two to identify mutations unique to the cancer. Candidate neoantigens are ranked by how strongly the resulting peptides are predicted to bind the patient's own major histocompatibility complex molecules, and by how much the corresponding genes are expressed. A construct encoding up to thirty-four selected neoantigens is then synthesised, sealed into lipid nanoparticles and injected, usually into muscle. The entire workflow, from tissue to injection, now takes roughly six to eight weeks when a manufacturing slot is available.
--- D
What follows is conventional immunology. Antigen-presenting cells take up the particles, translate the RNA and display the resulting peptides on their surface by both major histocompatibility pathways. These cells migrate to lymph nodes, where they prime CD4 and CD8 T cells that recognise the tumour. Cytotoxic T cells then seek out and destroy cells bearing the matching antigens, while memory cells persist and, in principle, patrol for relapse. The logic of combining the vaccine with a checkpoint inhibitor is that the tumour's local defences are suppressed at the same moment that new T cells are being raised against it.
--- E
The evidence has accumulated over several years. In the phase 2b KEYNOTE-942 trial, updated five-year follow-up presented in 2026 showed that adding the vaccine to pembrolizumab reduced the risk of recurrence or death by 49 per cent, with a hazard ratio of 0.510, and the risk of distant metastasis or death by 59 per cent, with a hazard ratio of 0.411. The benefit did not appear to depend on the tumour's expression of PD-L1, and no new safety signals emerged over five years, with adverse events comparable to those of the checkpoint inhibitor alone. Overall survival data, however, had not yet matured, and researchers caution that event-driven analyses in small personalised cohorts can overstate durability.
--- F
The binding constraint is increasingly industrial rather than scientific. Plants built during the pandemic were designed to make one or two antigens by the billion doses. Cancer vaccines invert the model: thousands of distinct sequences a year, each in small batches that must still meet full release testing. Trials in 2026 have paused enrolment not for want of patients but for want of manufacturing slots at contract organisations already committed to influenza, respiratory syncytial virus and next-generation preventive programmes. Timing compounds the problem: surgeons want patients vaccinated within weeks of resection, when any residual disease is most vulnerable, and eight weeks is often cited as the ceiling.
--- G
The field is also dividing. Personalised vaccines make most sense where the mutational burden is high, as in melanoma, whereas shared vaccines built from antigens common to a defined cancer can be manufactured in advance and given off the shelf, sacrificing precision for speed and scale. Beyond the Merck and Moderna alliance, BioNTech is pursuing both personalised and shared antigen approaches, while Gritstone bio and Genentech run competing programmes. Chinese groups such as CanSino have reported their own lipid nanoparticle systems and freeze-dried formulations. Several teams are applying artificial intelligence to the ranking step, where the choice of neoantigens determines much of the outcome.
--- H
Oncology is not the only destination. In July 2026 a candidate intended to achieve a functional cure for hepatitis B received clinical trial approvals in China, the United States and Laos and entered phase 1, the first mRNA therapeutic in that indication. Programmes against shingles, tuberculosis and respiratory syncytial virus are at earlier stages. Whether the platform fulfils its promise will depend less on the elegance of the biology than on the unglamorous work of shortening production, securing manufacturing capacity and demonstrating, in trials large enough to settle the question, that the survival curves hold.
